# 00. Project Overview

## Purpose

The Airport Employee AI Assistant is an intelligent system designed to provide airport staff with up-to-date information on service standards, internal regulations, security procedures, passenger handling, and other aspects of daily operations. The system answers employee questions based on an internal knowledge base built from corporate documentation.

## Bot Description

The AI Assistant is implemented as a chatbot accessible through the corporate network. It uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) technology to search the knowledge base for relevant information and generate accurate, context-aware responses. It understands natural language and interprets employee questions, providing fast access to the required information.

## Capabilities

* **Question Answering:** Provides information on passenger service rules, security, check-in, baggage, special categories of passengers, and other topics covered in the knowledge base.
* **Procedural Instructions:** Provides access to step-by-step instructions for performing various operations and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
* **Decision-Making Algorithms:** Supports decision-making in non-standard situations using built-in decision trees.
* **Documentation Search:** Quickly searches for specific terms, procedures, or policies within the knowledge base.
* **Operational Scenarios:** Provides examples of typical situations and recommended ways to resolve them.

## Limitations

* **Knowledge Scope:** Answers only questions covered by its knowledge base. Information outside the knowledge base is unavailable.
* **Confidentiality:** Does not process confidential information, passenger personal data, or information that is not intended for general access.
* **Legal Matters:** Does not provide legal advice or interpretations of legislation.
* **Technical Support:** Does not replace technical support for systems or equipment.
* **Real-Time Decisions:** May not reflect current operational information that has not yet been added to the knowledge base.

## Scope

* Passenger service standards.
* Check-in and boarding procedures.
* Baggage handling rules.
* Aviation security requirements.
* Passport and customs control procedures.
* Handling special categories of passengers.
* Response to irregular and emergency situations.
* Coordination between airport departments.
* Employee conduct and ethics standards.
* Privacy and data protection policies.

## Out of Scope

* Financial information.
* Employment contract details and compensation.
* Confidential investigation reports.
* Security incident details not included in the general knowledge base.
* Supplier and contract information.
* Legal information and interpretations of laws.
* Technical equipment manuals (unless included in the knowledge base as operational procedures).

## How to Use

1. Formulate your question in natural language.
2. Enter your question in the AI Assistant chat.
3. Wait for a response. The system will analyze your request, search the knowledge base for relevant information, and generate an answer.
4. If the answer is unclear or requires clarification, rephrase your question or ask follow-up questions.

## Example Questions

* "How do I check baggage for a child?"
* "What should I do if a passenger has no baggage but is booked on a flight?"
* "Which documents are required for passport control?"

### Examples of Good Queries

* "What are the steps to follow when a suspicious object is found in the check-in area?"
* "What measures are taken when a passenger arrives late for boarding?"
* "What are the requirements for transporting animals in the aircraft cabin?"

### Examples of Poor Queries

* "Tell me something interesting."
* "How are you?"
* "What is an airport?" (too general)

## What to Do If Your Question Is Outside the Knowledge Scope

If the AI Assistant cannot provide a satisfactory answer, it will let you know. In that case:

1. Make your question more specific.
2. Contact your direct supervisor or the appropriate airport department.
3. Report the missing information through the feedback system so the knowledge base can be expanded.

## Response Policy

* Responses are based on official internal airport documents and standards.
* The system strives for accuracy but does not guarantee absolute correctness.
* Responses are provided for reference purposes only and do not replace official instructions.

### Refusal to Answer

If a request concerns information outside the knowledge scope or involves confidential information, the system will politely refuse to provide the information and, when appropriate, direct the user to the appropriate source.

### Escalation to a Human Operator

In complex or emergency situations requiring human intervention, the system may recommend contacting an operator or the responsible person.

# 01. General Airport Operating Rules

## General Employee Responsibilities

### Purpose

To ensure a clear understanding of the core responsibilities of every airport employee in order to maintain high standards of service, safety, and operational efficiency.

### When It Applies

In all aspects of an airport employee's professional duties.

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees.

### Required Actions

* Follow all internal airport rules, policies, and standards.
* Perform assigned job responsibilities in accordance with your designated role.
* Maintain a high level of professionalism and ethical conduct.
* Ensure the safety of yourself, colleagues, passengers, and airport property.
* Report any incidents, violations, potential threats, or equipment malfunctions according to established procedures.
* Participate in all mandatory training sessions and assessments.
* Maintain a professional appearance in accordance with corporate standards.

### Exceptions

There are no general exceptions. These responsibilities apply to all employees.

### Special Cases

In emergency situations, responsibilities may be temporarily modified in accordance with emergency response plans.

### Procedure Completion

These responsibilities apply throughout the employee's working hours and conclude at the end of the shift or upon completion of a specific assigned task.

## Communication Standards

### Purpose

To ensure respectful, professional, and effective communication with all stakeholders, including passengers, colleagues, partners, and visitors.

### When It Applies

During all forms of communication (verbal, written, telephone, and digital).

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees.

### Required Actions

* Use a polite and respectful tone.
* Speak clearly and understandably.
* Practice active listening.
* Demonstrate friendliness and a willingness to help.
* Avoid jargon, slang, or ambiguous language when communicating with passengers.
* Remain calm and maintain self-control in stressful situations.
* Adapt your communication style to the cultural background and age of the person you are addressing.

### Exceptions

If there is a security threat or aggressive behavior from the other party, communication may be terminated or transferred to another employee or the appropriate service.

### Special Cases

If a language barrier exists, use interpreters, translation tools, gestures, or seek assistance from a colleague who speaks the required language.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure ends when the interaction with the individual or group has been completed.

## Document Handling

### Purpose

To ensure the proper handling, accuracy, confidentiality, and availability of documents.

### When It Applies

Whenever documents are received, processed, stored, transferred, or destroyed.

### Responsible Parties

All employees who have access to documents.

### Required Actions

* Verify the authenticity and validity of documents.
* Process documents in accordance with established procedures.
* Store documents securely to prevent unauthorized access.
* Use only officially approved forms and templates.
* Make records clearly and legibly.
* Monitor document validity periods and required updates.
* Destroy documents whose retention period has expired in accordance with the archive policy.

### Exceptions

Documents containing classified government information or other specially protected information must be handled under dedicated regulations.

### Special Cases

If counterfeit, damaged, or lost critical documents are discovered, immediately notify management and follow the appropriate response procedures.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once all required actions for the document (such as registration, transfer, or archiving) have been completed.

## Personal Data Protection

### Purpose

To ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data relating to passengers, employees, and third parties in accordance with applicable legislation.

### When It Applies

Whenever personal data is collected, processed, stored, transferred, or deleted.

### Responsible Parties

All employees with access to personal data.

### Required Actions

* Process personal data only for authorized purposes and on lawful grounds.
* Collect only the minimum amount of personal data required.
* Ensure data accuracy and keep information up to date.
* Store data in encrypted or otherwise protected form whenever possible.
* Restrict access based on the principle of least privilege.
* Do not disclose personal data to third parties without explicit authorization or a legal basis.
* Follow internal data protection policies and applicable external regulations.
* Report any personal data breach immediately.

### Exceptions

Processing may be permitted where required by law (for example, for security purposes or to comply with a court order).

### Special Cases

If a data breach is suspected, immediately isolate the source of the breach, notify the responsible personnel, and follow the emergency response procedure.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once personal data has been deleted or anonymized after the applicable retention period has expired.

## Ethics

### Purpose

To maintain high moral and professional standards of conduct among all airport employees.

### When It Applies

In all aspects of professional activity.

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees.

### Required Actions

* Act honestly, fairly, and in good faith.
* Do not engage in discrimination of any kind.
* Avoid conflicts of interest.
* Do not use your official position for personal gain.
* Respect the rights and dignity of all individuals.
* Comply with all applicable laws and internal policies.
* Report any instances of corruption, unethical conduct, or policy violations.

### Exceptions

There are no exceptions. Ethical principles are mandatory for all employees.

### Special Cases

When facing complex ethical dilemmas, seek guidance from management or the ethics office.

### Procedure Completion

Ethical principles apply continuously throughout an employee's career.

## Confidentiality

### Purpose

To protect confidential information belonging to the airport, its customers, and its partners from unauthorized disclosure.

### When It Applies

Whenever handling information that is classified as confidential or that is clearly intended to remain protected.

### Responsible Parties

All employees and contractors who have access to confidential information.

### Required Actions

* Recognize confidential information and understand its importance.
* Do not discuss confidential information outside the workplace or business context.
* Do not disclose confidential information to unauthorized persons.
* Use secure methods for storing and transmitting information.
* Follow the information classification policy.
* Protect devices and accounts containing confidential information.

### Exceptions

Disclosure may be permitted when legally required or with written authorization from an authorized individual.

### Special Cases

If a confidentiality breach is suspected, immediately notify management and the Information Security department.

### Procedure Completion

Confidentiality obligations continue even after employment has ended.

## Safety Standards

### Purpose

To ensure the physical safety of everyone within the airport and protect airport infrastructure from threats.

### When It Applies

Throughout the airport and during any safety-related activities.

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees.

### Required Actions

* Follow all aviation security protocols.
* Participate in regular safety inspections and training.
* Report suspicious objects or behavior.
* Comply with access control procedures.
* Use personal protective equipment (PPE) where required.
* Participate in emergency drills and inspections.
* Never ignore potential threats.

### Exceptions

There are no exceptions. Safety is always the highest priority.

### Special Cases

If a security threat is identified, immediately activate the appropriate alarm and follow the established emergency response plan.

### Procedure Completion

Safety standards apply continuously, 24/7.

## Employee Accountability

### Purpose

To define the levels of individual and collective responsibility for performing assigned duties, complying with policies, and accepting the consequences of violations.

### When It Applies

During the performance of all professional responsibilities.

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees.

### Required Actions

* Take personal responsibility for your work.
* Follow corporate values and policies.
* Report problems and incidents promptly.
* Use airport resources responsibly and efficiently.
* Maintain professional competence.
* Follow management instructions.
* Understand that your actions can affect the airport's reputation.

### Exceptions

Responsibility may be partially or fully waived in cases of force majeure or life-saving actions performed in accordance with approved procedures.

### Special Cases

If an error or incident occurs, report it honestly and cooperate fully with any investigation.

### Procedure Completion

Employee accountability remains in effect throughout the period of employment and may continue after employment has ended.

## Greeting Passengers

To ensure a friendly, professional, and informative first impression for passengers upon entering the airport. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### When It Applies

During the first interaction with a passenger on airport premises (at the entrance, reception area, or waiting lounges).

### Responsible Parties

Employees who are visible to passengers (reception staff, customer assistance, security personnel, cleaning staff, ticketing agents).

### Required Actions

* Greet the passenger politely and warmly ("Welcome!", "Good morning/afternoon!").

## Providing Information

### Purpose

To provide passengers with accurate, up-to-date, and complete information in response to their inquiries.

### When It Applies

Whenever a passenger requests information.

### Responsible Parties

Any employee capable of answering the question, including specialized services (information desk, customer service, airline representatives).

### Required Actions

* Listen carefully to the passenger's question.
* Verify the accuracy of the information before providing it.
* Use clear and simple language.
* Confirm understanding: "If I understand correctly, you're asking about...?"
* Provide all relevant related information (for example, if the question concerns boarding, also mention transfer procedures if applicable).
* If the question is outside your area of responsibility, direct the passenger to the appropriate specialist or department.
* Ensure the passenger has received all the required information and no longer needs assistance.

### Exceptions

Information cannot be provided if it is unavailable or confidential.

### Special Cases

If the passenger does not understand the explanation, rephrase it or use visual aids such as flight information displays or terminal maps.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure ends after confirming that the passenger has received the requested information.

## Wayfinding

### Purpose

To help passengers find the required location within the airport (check-in counter, boarding gate, restroom, restaurant, etc.).

### When It Applies

When a passenger asks for directions or appears to be lost.

### Responsible Parties

All employees familiar with the airport layout.

### Required Actions

* Clearly explain the route using recognizable landmarks ("Walk straight to the fountain, then turn right...").
* Point out directional signs and gate or zone indicators.
* Escort the passenger if necessary.
* Confirm that the passenger understands the directions.
* Offer alternative routes if the primary route is unavailable.

### Exceptions

If the passenger declines assistance or the route is inaccessible due to security restrictions.

### Special Cases

For passengers with reduced mobility, use designated accessible routes and PRM assistance services.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the passenger reaches the destination or clearly understands how to get there.

## Queue Management

### Purpose

To ensure fair and efficient service for passengers waiting in line.

### When It Applies

In all areas where queues form (check-in, security screening, passport control, baggage claim).

### Responsible Parties

Employees responsible for passenger service points.

### Required Actions

* Maintain order in the queue.
* Inform passengers of the estimated waiting time whenever possible.
* Follow the first-come, first-served principle except for priority passenger categories.
* Provide priority service to VIPs, PRM passengers, families with young children, pregnant women, and other eligible categories.
* Keep passengers informed about delays or operational changes.
* Ensure a comfortable waiting environment (sufficient space, access to information displays).

### Exceptions

Priority passenger categories are served ahead of the queue in accordance with established procedures.

### Special Cases

If queues become excessively long, notify management so that additional resources can be allocated.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure ends once all passengers in the queue have been served.

## Handling Complaints

### Purpose

To handle passenger complaints professionally and effectively while aiming for a satisfactory resolution.

### When It Applies

Whenever a passenger expresses dissatisfaction regarding service, delays, lost baggage, or similar issues.

The employee approached by the passenger or the Customer Service department.

### Required Actions

* Express empathy and understanding: "I'm very sorry you've experienced this."

### Special Cases

# 03. Passenger Check-in

## Standard Check-in

### Purpose

To ensure accurate and efficient passenger check-in, including document verification, baggage acceptance, and issuance of a boarding pass. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### When It Applies

During the standard pre-departure check-in process.

### Responsible Parties

Check-in agents, airline personnel, and passenger service agents.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm the passenger's reservation using their name and Passenger Name Record (PNR).
2. Verify the validity of the passenger's identification document and ticket.
3. Confirm that the name on the travel document matches the name in the reservation.
4. Verify that all required visas and travel authorizations for the destination country are available.
5. Accept checked baggage, if any, and verify compliance with baggage allowance rules.
6. Assign a seat if one has not already been selected.
7. Issue a boarding pass showing the flight number, date, departure time, boarding gate, and seat assignment.
8. Inform the passenger of the check-in and boarding closing times.
9. Direct the passenger to the security screening area and explain any required next steps.

### Exceptions

If the passenger has already completed online check-in and has no checked baggage, the process is expedited.

### Special Cases

If the passenger's name does not match the reservation, verify whether the discrepancy is due to an error and request supporting documentation or correction of the booking.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the boarding pass has been issued and the passenger has been directed to the security screening area.

---

## Online Check-in

### Purpose

To allow passengers to check in online before arriving at the airport, saving time during departure.

### When It Applies

When the passenger checks in through the airline's website or mobile application.

### Responsible Parties

The passenger (self-service) and airline support staff.

### Required Actions

1. Access the airline's website or mobile application.
2. Enter the reservation details (name, PNR, or ticket number).
3. Confirm or select a seat if available.
4. Receive an electronic boarding pass (PDF or mobile version).
5. Arrive at the airport with a valid identification document and the confirmed boarding pass.
6. Proceed through security screening and boarding.

### Exceptions

Passengers requiring special assistance (for example, PRM passengers) or traveling with baggage that requires special handling may need additional processing at the check-in counter.

### Special Cases

If online check-in is unavailable due to technical issues or airline restrictions, the passenger should check in at the airport counter.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the boarding pass has been issued and the passenger arrives at the airport.

---

## Automatic Check-in

### Purpose

To automatically check in passengers who have not completed check-in manually within the designated time window.

### When It Applies

When a passenger has not checked in online or at the airport, and the airline supports automatic check-in.

### Responsible Parties

The airline's automated systems and check-in staff.

### Required Actions

1. The airline system automatically checks in the passenger at the scheduled time before departure (typically 24–48 hours).
2. Assign an available or randomly selected seat.
3. Deliver the boarding pass by email, SMS, or the airline application.
4. The passenger must arrive at the airport with a valid identification document and boarding pass.

### Exceptions

Automatic check-in is not available if the passenger has not provided contact information or if the airline does not support this service.

### Special Cases

If the automatically assigned seat is not acceptable, the passenger may request a seat change at the check-in counter, subject to airline policy.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the boarding pass has been sent and the passenger arrives at the airport.

## Document Verification

### Purpose

To verify the authenticity and validity of all travel documents required for the journey.

### When It Applies

During check-in and passport control.

### Responsible Parties

Check-in agents and passport control officers.

### Required Actions

1. Verify the authenticity of the passport or identity document using both visual inspection and available technical verification tools.
2. Compare the passenger's appearance with the photograph in the document.
3. Check the document's expiration date.
4. Verify that the name matches the reservation.
5. Confirm the validity of any required visa.
6. Check any additional required documentation (medical certificates, permits, etc.).

### Exceptions

Simplified document verification procedures may apply for certain countries or routes.

### Special Cases

If a forged document or discrepancy is identified, deny check-in and immediately notify the appropriate authorities (Airport Security, police, or other competent agencies).

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once all required documents have been successfully verified.

## Overbooking

### Purpose

To manage situations where the number of confirmed passengers exceeds the available seats on the aircraft.

### When It Applies

When the number of checked-in passengers exceeds the aircraft's seating capacity.

### Responsible Parties

Airline personnel, overbooking coordinators, and passenger service agents.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that the flight has been oversold.
2. Inform passengers about the situation.
3. Request volunteers willing to give up their seats in exchange for compensation (such as refunds, travel vouchers, hotel accommodation, or meals).
4. If there are insufficient volunteers, deny boarding according to established airline policies (often affecting the last passengers to check in or passengers without checked baggage).
5. Rebook affected passengers on alternative flights.
6. Provide compensation and explain the passenger's rights.

### Exceptions

Passengers with certain statuses (such as elite frequent flyer members) may be protected from involuntary denied boarding.

### Special Cases

If a passenger refuses to comply with an involuntary denied boarding decision, follow the airline's established removal procedures.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once all remaining confirmed passengers have boarded the flight.

## Late Check-in

### Purpose

To manage passengers who arrive at the check-in counter after check-in has closed.

### When It Applies

When a passenger arrives after the published check-in closing time.

### Responsible Parties

Check-in staff and airline personnel.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that check-in has already closed.

### Purpose

To determine the maximum permitted baggage limits (weight, dimensions, and number of pieces) for different travel classes and flight types. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

### When It Applies

When checked baggage is accepted at the check-in counter or inspected at the airport entrance.

## Carry-on Baggage

### Purpose

To determine the permitted size, weight, and contents of items that passengers may bring into the aircraft cabin.

### When It Applies

During security screening and before boarding.

### Responsible Parties

The passenger, security screening personnel, and airline staff.

### Required Actions

### Exceptions

Certain items (such as medications, baby food, and food required for diabetic passengers) are permitted in reasonable quantities but may require additional inspection.

If the carry-on baggage exceeds size or weight limits, the passenger may be required to check it into the aircraft hold.

The procedure is complete once the passenger has successfully passed security screening with permitted carry-on baggage.

# 05. Aviation Security

## Security Screening

### Purpose

To ensure the safety of passengers, crew, and the aircraft by detecting prohibited items and hazardous substances.

### When It Applies

At the entrance to the airport security screening area before boarding.

### Responsible Parties

Airport security officers, screening personnel, and the airline.

### Required Actions

1. The passenger presents their boarding pass and identification document.
2. The passenger places carry-on baggage and metallic objects into screening trays.
3. The trays pass through the X-ray scanner.
4. The passenger passes through a metal detector or body scanner.
5. If an alarm is triggered, conduct additional screening (pat-down, secondary inspection, or clarification).
6. Electronic devices may require separate inspection.
7. After successfully completing screening, direct the passenger to the departure waiting area.

### Exceptions

Certain passenger categories (such as airport personnel or VIPs with authorized special status) may be subject to simplified screening procedures.

### Special Cases

If a passenger refuses to undergo security screening, they must be denied access to the secure area and boarding.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the passenger has successfully completed all screening procedures and entered the secure departure area.

---

## Prohibited Items

### Purpose

To prevent prohibited items that pose a security risk from being brought on board an aircraft.

### When It Applies

During the inspection of checked baggage and carry-on baggage at check-in and security screening.

### Responsible Parties

Passengers, security personnel, check-in staff, and airline personnel.

### Required Actions

1. Review the list of prohibited items (such as sharp objects, explosives, firearms, gas canisters, and fuel).
2. Inspect checked baggage and carry-on baggage for prohibited items.
3. If a prohibited item is found, confiscate it.
4. Explain to the passenger why the item has been confiscated.
5. Inform the passenger whether the item can be shipped separately (for example, as cargo) or transported by another approved method.
6. Record the incident.

### Exceptions

Certain restricted items (such as medications and batteries) are permitted under specific conditions and quantity limits.

### Special Cases

If a passenger intentionally attempts to carry a prohibited item, the incident may result in legal action.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the prohibited item has been removed and an appropriate decision has been made regarding further action.

---

## Suspicious Items

### Purpose

To ensure safety through the timely detection, isolation, and assessment of potentially dangerous items.

### When It Applies

When an unidentified or suspicious item is discovered in a public area of the airport.

### Responsible Parties

Any employee who discovers the item, Airport Security, the police, and the bomb disposal unit (EOD).

### Required Actions

1. Do not touch the item.
2. Report the discovery immediately to Airport Security or the operations control center.
3. Provide the exact location and a description of the item.
4. Establish a safety perimeter around the item if it is safe to do so.
5. Prevent unauthorized persons from approaching the area.
6. Wait for the arrival of the appropriate specialists (Airport Security, police, or the bomb disposal unit).
7. Follow all instructions provided by the responding specialists.
8. Document the incident.

### Exceptions

If the item clearly poses no threat (for example, a lost phone or wallet), process it according to the airport's lost property procedures.

### Special Cases

If there is a potential bomb threat, partial or full evacuation of the area may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete after specialists have assessed the item and determined the appropriate course of action (neutralization, removal, or confirmation that the item is safe).

---

## Access Control

### Purpose

To ensure that only authorized personnel have access to restricted areas, critical airport facilities, and aircraft.

### When It Applies

When entering or leaving security-restricted areas, the apron, warehouses, administrative offices, or flight crew areas.

### Responsible Parties

Security officers, personnel with access badges, and Airport Security.

### Required Actions

1. Establish access control checkpoints.
2. Verify the validity of access badges and identification documents.
3. Inspect employees' bags and personal belongings where required.
4. Record entries and exits when applicable.
5. Prevent unauthorized persons from entering restricted areas without an authorized escort.
6. Use technical security systems (badge readers, surveillance cameras, etc.).

### Exceptions

In emergency situations (for example, urgent medical response), access control procedures may be temporarily relaxed.

### Special Cases

If a counterfeit access badge or unauthorized access attempt is suspected, immediately detain the individual where authorized and notify Airport Security.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once access has been verified and granted or denied.

---

## Security Perimeter

### Purpose

To isolate a designated area in order to ensure safety, support an investigation, or manage an emergency situation.

### When It Applies

When a suspicious item is discovered, a security threat exists, an emergency occurs, or emergency services are conducting operations.

### Responsible Parties

Airport Security, police, fire services, and specialized response teams.

### Required Actions

1. Define the boundaries of the hazardous area.
2. Establish physical barriers (security tape, fencing, or other barriers).
3. Deploy security personnel or police officers to control the perimeter.
4. Evacuate people from the restricted area.
5. Prevent unauthorized access.
6. Notify other airport services and the operations control center.
7. Follow the instructions of the incident commander.

### Exceptions

There are no exceptions. Security perimeters must be established whenever required.

### Special Cases

The size of the perimeter may vary depending on the nature of the threat (for example, chemical hazards or explosive devices).

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the threat has been eliminated and the security perimeter has been officially removed.

## Security Violators

### Purpose

To ensure airport safety by identifying and responding to individuals who violate airport security rules.

### When It Applies

In cases of aggressive behavior, unauthorized access attempts, threats, or violations of airport security procedures.

### Responsible Parties

Airport Security, police, security guards, and airport personnel.

### Required Actions

1. Identify the violation.
2. Assess the level of risk.
3. Attempt to stop the individual through verbal communication.
4. If necessary and permitted, physically restrain the individual.
5. Notify Airport Security or the police.
6. Record the incident (video, witness statements, and written report).
7. Ensure the safety of other passengers and personnel.

### Exceptions

Individuals posing an immediate threat must be detained without delay.

### Special Cases

Large-scale disturbances may require additional security resources and the evacuation of part of the airport.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the individual has been detained and transferred to the appropriate authorities.

## Security Threat

### Purpose

To identify and respond to any information or event that could threaten the safety of the airport, aircraft, or passengers.

### When It Applies

Upon receiving threats (verbal, written, telephone, or online), observing suspicious activity, or discovering devices that may indicate a potential threat.

### Responsible Parties

All airport employees, Airport Security, police, airlines, and airport operations control services.

### Required Actions

1. Receive and document information about the threat.
2. Assess the seriousness and credibility of the threat.
3. Activate the appropriate threat response level in accordance with the airport's security response plan.
4. Notify the relevant authorities (Airport Security, police, FSD, and government agencies).
5. Implement protective measures for potential targets (evacuation, security perimeter, additional screening, etc.).
6. Coordinate activities with all involved agencies.
7. Document all actions taken.

### Exceptions

False alarms must also be investigated, although the response level may be adjusted as appropriate.

### Special Cases

A threat may target a specific flight, terminal, or the entire airport, affecting the scope of the response.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the threat has been eliminated or determined to be unfounded and normal airport operations have resumed.

---

## Security Escalation

### Purpose

To manage situations in which the level of a security threat increases and requires enhanced protective measures.

### When It Applies

When standard security measures are no longer sufficient or the threat level increases.

### Responsible Parties

Airport management, Airport Security, police, and government authorities.

### Required Actions

1. Assess the current situation and determine whether enhanced security measures are required.
2. Activate the next level of the airport security protocol.
3. Notify the relevant authorities and affected airlines.
4. Introduce enhanced screening procedures and additional access restrictions.
5. Suspend airport or flight operations if necessary.
6. Maintain effective communication among all responding agencies.
7. Keep passengers and airport personnel informed.

### Exceptions

Security escalation may be initiated by order of government authorities.

### Special Cases

Escalation may extend to other airports or airlines depending on the nature of the threat.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the threat level has been reduced and the airport has returned to the previous security level.

# 06. Passport and Customs Control

## General Procedures

### Purpose

To verify the authenticity of a passenger's travel documents, confirm their eligibility to enter or leave the country, and ensure compliance with immigration laws.

### When It Applies

When crossing an international border (departure from or arrival into a country).

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, customs authorities, and airlines (document verification before boarding).

### Required Actions

1. The passenger presents a valid passport.
2. The border officer verifies the authenticity of the passport using both visual inspection and technical verification tools.
3. Verify the passport's validity period (it is typically required to remain valid for at least six months).
4. Confirm that the name in the passport matches the reservation.
5. Verify the presence of a valid visa, if required.
6. Confirm the purpose of travel and intended length of stay.
7. Apply the required passport or visa stamps or endorsements where applicable.
8. Direct the passenger to customs if required.

### Exceptions

Citizens of EU/Schengen countries may not require a visa in certain cases. Simplified procedures may apply to transit passengers.

### Special Cases

The border officer may ask additional questions regarding the purpose of the visit, financial means, or accommodation.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the travel documents have been verified and permission to enter or leave the country has been granted.

---

## Document Errors

### Purpose

To manage situations where an error is identified in a passport or visa.

### When It Applies

When discrepancies are found in the passenger's name, date of birth, passport number, or other personal information.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, consulates, and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Determine the type of error (typographical error, incorrect spelling, mismatch with the reservation, etc.).
2. Verify whether the error originated during the document issuance process.
3. Compare the document with other forms of identification (such as the airline ticket or driver's license).
4. Contact the issuing authority or consulate if necessary.
5. Decide whether to allow entry with clarification, require correction of the document, or deny entry.

### Exceptions

If the error is clearly an official typographical mistake, reasonable discretion may be applied where permitted by law.

### Special Cases

If the border officer is unable to make a decision, consultation with a senior officer may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once a decision has been made regarding entry or exit.

---

## Refusal of Entry or Exit

### Purpose

To manage situations in which a passenger is denied permission to enter or leave a country.

### When It Applies

When legal grounds for refusal exist under applicable immigration legislation.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, immigration courts, and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Determine the legal basis for refusal (missing visa, expired passport, entry ban, suspected legal violation, etc.).
2. Clearly and politely explain the reason for the refusal.
3. Inform the passenger that they are not permitted to cross the border.
4. Arrange for the passenger's return to the country of departure (at the airline's or passenger's expense, as applicable).
5. Record the incident.
6. Notify the airline that the passenger is not authorized for boarding or entry.

### Exceptions

A refusal must never be based on discriminatory grounds.

### Special Cases

A passenger may appeal the decision, although opportunities to do so at the airport are generally limited.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the passenger has been returned to the country of departure.

---

## Visas

### Purpose

To verify that a passenger holds a valid visa when one is required for entry into the destination country.

### When It Applies

When entering a country that requires a visa.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, consulates, and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Determine whether a visa is required for the destination country.
2. Verify that the visa is present in the passport.
3. Confirm that the visa is valid on the intended date of entry.
4. Verify that the visa type (tourist, business, student, etc.) matches the purpose of travel.
5. Verify the permitted number of entries (single-entry, multiple-entry, etc.).
6. Ensure that the visa has not been canceled, revoked, or damaged.

### Exceptions

Some countries allow visa-free travel or accept electronic visas (e-Visas).

### Special Cases

Certain visas require supporting documentation, such as medical insurance or hotel reservations.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the visa has been confirmed as valid and entry has been authorized.

## Transit

### Purpose

To process passengers traveling through a country in transit and determine whether a transit visa is required.

### When It Applies

For passengers passing through a country without remaining there, whether staying airside or entering landside where applicable.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Determine whether a transit visa is required.
2. Verify the passenger's transit visa if required.
3. Determine whether the passenger will remain in the transit area or leave the airport (airside vs. landside transit).
4. Verify that the passenger holds a confirmed onward ticket.
5. Confirm that the planned transit duration complies with applicable limits.

### Exceptions

Many countries permit visa-free transit under specific conditions, such as maximum transit time and possession of a confirmed onward ticket.

### Special Cases

If the passenger intends to leave the airport during transit, a standard entry visa may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the required documents have been verified and transit has been authorized.

---

## Deportation

### Purpose

To manage the enforced removal of a foreign national from the country in accordance with a lawful decision issued by the competent authorities.

### When It Applies

In cases involving violations of immigration law, security concerns, visa overstays, or other legal grounds.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, immigration authorities, police, and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Receive the official deportation order.
2. Take the individual into custody as required by law.
3. Arrange escorted transportation to the aircraft.
4. Escort the individual on board and transfer custody to the receiving authorities where applicable.
5. Document the deportation process.
6. Notify the airline of any special handling requirements.

### Exceptions

Deportation procedures are strictly governed by applicable laws and regulations.

### Special Cases

The deportation process may involve legal proceedings and appeals.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the individual has physically departed the country.

---

## Special Cases

### Purpose

To manage unusual or exceptional situations arising during passport control procedures.

### When It Applies

Whenever circumstances fall outside standard operating procedures.

### Responsible Parties

Border control authorities, airport management, and designated special review authorities.

### Required Actions

1. Assess the unique circumstances of the case.
2. Consult with supervisors or legal advisors as necessary.
3. Follow any applicable special procedures or protocols.
4. Document the case.
5. Ensure compliance with the law while maintaining security.
6. Make a reasonable and legally supported decision based on all available information.

### Exceptions

All actions must comply with the laws and regulations of the country concerned.

### Special Cases

Examples include the loss of a passport during transit, the use of a temporary travel document, or situations involving diplomatic immunity.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once an appropriate decision has been made and implemented.

**FAQ**

**Question:** What is required to pass passport control?  
**Answer:** A valid passport, a visa if required, and a boarding pass.

**Question:** Can I enter a country with an expired passport?  
**Answer:** No. Your passport must remain valid for the entire duration of your stay and, in many cases, for at least six months beyond your intended entry date.

**Question:** What should I do if there is an error in my passport?  
**Answer:** Explain the situation to the authorities. You may need to have the passport corrected before traveling.

**Question:** Do I need a visa for transit?  
**Answer:** It depends on the transit country. Many countries allow visa-free transit under certain conditions.

**Question:** What is deportation?  
**Answer:** Deportation is the compulsory removal of a foreign national from a country by order of the competent authorities.

**Question:** What should I do if I am refused entry?  
**Answer:** In most cases, you will be returned on the next available flight. You may have the right to appeal the decision, although this usually cannot be done at the airport.

**Question:** Can I pass passport control without a visa if one is not required?  
**Answer:** Yes. For visa-free destinations, a visa is not required, but your passport and other travel documents will still be checked.

**Question:** What is checked during passport control?  
**Answer:** Officers verify the authenticity of your passport, its validity, visa status, consistency of your personal information, and the purpose of your visit.

**Question:** Can I change my entry date if it is specified in my visa?  
**Answer:** A visa is valid only during the period stated on the visa. Entry is generally permitted on any date within that validity period unless additional restrictions apply.

**Question:** What should I do if I lose my passport at the airport?  
**Answer:** Immediately report the loss to the police and contact your country's consulate or embassy to obtain an emergency travel document.

# 07. Boarding

## Boarding Preparation

### Purpose

To ensure that all systems, personnel, and passengers are ready for safe and efficient boarding of the aircraft.

### When It Applies

Before boarding begins for a flight.

### Responsible Parties

The airline, flight crew, jet bridge or shuttle bus personnel, operations dispatchers, and gate agents.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that the flight is ready for boarding (the aircraft is at the gate, the jet bridge or shuttle bus is ready, the crew is on board, and baggage has been loaded).
2. Establish the boarding start and boarding close times.
3. Prepare boarding announcements (flight number, gate, and boarding time).
4. Ensure gate personnel are present and ready.
5. Prepare boarding pass validation systems.
6. Ensure the aircraft cabin is ready (cleaning completed, bedding and supplies prepared where applicable, cabin crew ready).

### Exceptions

Boarding preparation may be delayed due to flight delays, technical issues, or adverse weather conditions.

### Special Cases

If the aircraft is still occupied by passengers from the previous flight, preparation begins immediately after the aircraft has been cleared.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the boarding announcement has been made and all relevant services have confirmed readiness.

---

## Boarding Control

### Purpose

To verify passengers' boarding passes and travel documents before they board the aircraft.

### When It Applies

As passengers enter the jet bridge or shuttle bus leading to the aircraft.

### Responsible Parties

Gate agents, airline personnel, and passenger service agents.

### Required Actions

1. The passenger presents a boarding pass and identification document where required.
2. Verify that the boarding pass corresponds to the correct flight, date, and departure time.
3. Confirm that the passenger has not been removed from the flight (for example, because of overbooking).
4. Determine whether the passenger is a VIP or a Passenger with Reduced Mobility (PRM) requiring special assistance.
5. Direct the passenger to the jet bridge or shuttle bus.
6. Monitor the passenger count to ensure it matches the aircraft's seating capacity.

### Exceptions

Identification documents may not be required for certain domestic flights.

### Special Cases

If the boarding pass is invalid or the passenger is not listed in the boarding system, boarding must be denied.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the boarding pass has been verified and the passenger has been directed to the aircraft.

---

## Priority Boarding

### Purpose

To allow designated categories of passengers to board first for operational efficiency and special assistance.

### When It Applies

At the beginning of the boarding process.

### Responsible Parties

Gate agents and airline personnel.

### Required Actions

1. Announce the start of priority boarding.
2. Board the following passenger categories first:
   * Passengers with Reduced Mobility (PRM).
   * Passengers traveling with young children.
   * Business Class passengers and eligible frequent flyer status holders (subject to airline policy).
   * VIP passengers.
3. Provide escort assistance where required.
4. Verify boarding passes.

### Exceptions

Priority categories may vary depending on the airline's boarding policy.

### Special Cases

If a priority passenger arrives after priority boarding has ended, they may lose priority boarding privileges.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once all eligible priority passengers have boarded.

---

## Late Passengers

### Purpose

To manage passengers who arrive at the boarding gate after boarding has closed.

### When It Applies

When a passenger arrives after the boarding gate has officially closed.

### Responsible Parties

Gate agents, airline personnel, and operations dispatchers.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that boarding has officially closed.
2. Inform the passenger that boarding has ended.
3. Explain that the passenger can no longer board the flight.
4. Arrange rebooking on the next available flight.
5. Explain any applicable fees or fare differences.

### Exceptions

If the flight has been delayed by the airline, passengers may still be accepted after the original boarding deadline.

### Special Cases

In some situations, if the aircraft has not yet departed from the gate, the passenger may still be accepted for boarding.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once boarding has been denied and the passenger has been rebooked where applicable.

---

## Passenger Removal

### Purpose

To remove a passenger from the aircraft before departure for legitimate operational, safety, or medical reasons.

### When It Applies

When a security threat, disruptive behavior, medical condition, or overbooking requires the passenger to leave the aircraft before departure.

### Responsible Parties

The flight crew, airline personnel, and Airport Security.

### Required Actions

1. Determine the reason for the passenger's removal (security, behavior, medical condition, or overbooking).
2. Explain the reason to the passenger.
3. If the removal is related to security, contact Airport Security.
4. Assist the passenger in leaving the aircraft.
5. Retrieve or invalidate the boarding pass as required.
6. Arrange an alternative flight or refund where appropriate.
7. Record the incident.

### Exceptions

Passenger removal is generally permitted only before the aircraft doors have been closed and the aircraft has departed from the gate.

### Special Cases

If the passenger refuses to comply, assistance from Airport Security or law enforcement may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the passenger has left the aircraft and all required administrative actions have been completed.

---

## Flight Closure

### Purpose

To complete the boarding process and prepare the aircraft for departure.

### When It Applies

After boarding has closed and all confirmed passengers are on board.

### Responsible Parties

The flight crew, airline personnel, operations dispatchers, and gate agents.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm the final passenger count.
2. Reconcile the passenger manifest with the scanned boarding passes.
3. Close the aircraft doors.
4. Prepare departure documentation (passenger manifest, weight and balance documentation).
5. Disconnect and retract the jet bridge if applicable.
6. Transfer operational control to Air Traffic Control (ATC) for pushback, taxi, and departure.

### Exceptions

Flight closure may be delayed because of technical issues or adverse weather conditions.

### Special Cases

If a passenger who is recorded as boarded cannot be located on the aircraft, a complete reconciliation and additional security verification may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is complete once the aircraft doors have been closed and the aircraft has begun taxiing toward the runway.

---

**FAQ**

**Question:** When does boarding begin?  
**Answer:** Boarding usually begins 30–60 minutes before departure. The exact boarding time is announced at the airport and shown on the boarding pass.

**Question:** Who boards first?  
**Answer:** Priority boarding is generally offered to PRM passengers, passengers traveling with young children, Business Class passengers, and eligible frequent flyer status holders, depending on the airline's policy.

**Question:** What should I do if I arrive late for boarding?  
**Answer:** You will normally not be allowed to board. Contact an airline representative to discuss available rebooking options.

**Question:** Can I board without a boarding pass?  
**Answer:** No. A valid boarding pass is required to board the aircraft.

**Question:** What is flight closure?  
**Answer:** Flight closure is the point at which boarding has been completed, the aircraft doors are closed, and the aircraft is prepared for departure.

**Question:** Why might a passenger be removed from a flight?  
**Answer:** A passenger may be removed for security reasons, disruptive behavior, medical concerns, or operational reasons such as overbooking.

**Question:** Do I need to show my passport during boarding?  
**Answer:** Usually not, although identification or a passport may be required for international flights or when requested by the airline.

**Question:** How are boarding passes verified?  
**Answer:** Staff verify the flight number, departure date and time, passenger name, and boarding status using the airline's boarding system.

**Question:** How long does boarding usually last?  
**Answer:** Boarding typically lasts 30–45 minutes, after which the boarding gate is closed.

**Question:** What happens if I do not board my flight?  
**Answer:** Your reservation may be canceled, and you may need to purchase a new ticket or pay any applicable rebooking fees, depending on your fare conditions.

# 08. Special Categories of Passengers

## PRM (Passengers with Reduced Mobility)

### Purpose

Ensure equal access to air travel and provide appropriate assistance to passengers with reduced mobility.

### When It Applies

When a passenger indicates the need for special assistance during booking or check-in.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, airport staff, and specialized assistance agents.

### Required Actions

1. During booking, the passenger specifies the type of assistance required (WC, WCHS, WCHC, etc.).
2. Confirm the booking with a PRM designation.
3. Provide access to mobility equipment (wheelchairs).
4. Ensure assistance throughout all stages (check-in, security screening, boarding, and deplaning).
5. Assist with boarding the aircraft and seating the passenger.
6. Ensure proper storage and securing of the wheelchair (if it is non-foldable).
7. Provide assistance with restroom access if required and feasible.
8. Upon arrival, provide the same level of assistance during deplaning.

### Exceptions

Assistance is provided within the capabilities of the airport and the airline.

### Special Cases

If assistance requires medical equipment or medical personnel, prior approval and additional arrangements may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the passenger has safely and comfortably completed their journey.

## Children

### Purpose

Ensure the safety and comfort of children during air travel.

### When It Applies

When transporting passengers under 12 years of age.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, parents/guardians, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Determine the child's age (under 2 years, 2–12 years).
2. Verify documents (birth certificate, passport).
3. Confirm that the child is traveling with a parent/guardian or under the UMNR program.
4. Provide appropriate seating (including a child restraint seat if required).
5. Offer priority boarding for young children.
6. Inform parents about child safety regulations.

### Exceptions

Children under 2 years of age may travel on an adult's lap free of charge, subject to age verification.

### Special Cases

If the child is traveling alone, strict UMNR (Unaccompanied Minor) procedures apply.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the child and parent/guardian have boarded, or once the child has been handed over under the UMNR program.

## Unaccompanied Minors (UMNR)

### Purpose

Ensure the safety and proper supervision of children traveling without adults.

### When It Applies

When a child between 5 and 12 years of age travels without parents or legal guardians.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, parents/guardians, airport staff, and cabin crew.

### Required Actions

1. Arrange the UMNR service with the airline in advance.
2. The parent/guardian accompanies the child to the check-in counter.
3. An airport staff member escorts the child to the boarding gate.
4. During the flight, the child is supervised by the cabin crew.
5. Upon arrival, the child is released only to the authorized person (who must present identification).

### Exceptions

Some airlines do not accept unaccompanied children under 5 years of age.

### Special Cases

If the authorized pickup person does not arrive, the child remains under staff supervision until the situation is resolved.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the child has been handed over to the authorized person at the destination.

## Pregnant Passengers

### Purpose

Ensure the safety of both the mother and the unborn child while taking pregnancy-related considerations into account.

### When It Applies

When a passenger is pregnant.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, the passenger, and medical personnel (if necessary).

### Required Actions

1. Determine the stage of pregnancy.
2. Verify whether a medical certificate is required (typically after 28 weeks).
3. Provide a comfortable seat (possibly with additional legroom).
4. Offer priority boarding.
5. Inform the passenger about applicable policies and any potential need for medical assistance.
6. In the event of threatened miscarriage or pregnancy complications, provide first aid and request medical assistance.

### Exceptions

Some airlines do not transport pregnant passengers after 36 weeks (32 weeks for multiple pregnancies).

### Special Cases

If pregnancy complications arise, medical consultation or denial of travel may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the flight has been completed safely.

## Elderly Passengers

### Purpose

Provide comfort and the necessary assistance to elderly passengers.

### When It Applies

When transporting elderly passengers, especially those requiring additional assistance.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, airport staff, and the passenger.

### Required Actions

1. Determine whether the passenger requires additional assistance.
2. Provide priority service (check-in, security screening, boarding).
3. Offer assistance with airport navigation.
4. Provide comfortable seating on board.
5. Take possible medical needs into account.

### Exceptions

Assistance is provided upon request or when clearly necessary.

### Special Cases

If an elderly passenger requires continuous medical care, an accompanying person or a medical certificate may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the passenger has safely and comfortably completed the flight.

All procedures must comply with applicable safety and security regulations.

# 09. Irregular Operations

## Flight Delay

### Purpose

Ensure passengers are informed and flight operations are properly managed when a departure is delayed.

### When It Applies

When a flight does not depart at its scheduled time.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, dispatch services, airport staff, and ground handling agents.

### Required Actions

1. Determine the reason for the delay (weather, technical issues, crew availability, air traffic control).
2. Update flight information displays and reservation systems.
3. Make an announcement with the updated departure time.
4. Provide passengers with updates (SMS, email, mobile app).
5. For extended delays (typically more than 2 hours), provide food and beverages.
6. For very long delays (typically more than 4–5 hours, depending on the schedule), arrange hotel accommodation if applicable.
7. Continue updating passengers as new information becomes available.
8. Prepare passengers for possible boarding changes or ticket rebooking/refunds.

### Exceptions

Weather conditions or government actions often exempt the airline from compensation obligations.

### Special Cases

If the delay is caused by issues on a previous flight segment, the airline should notify passengers in advance.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the flight departs or is canceled and passengers have been rebooked or refunded.

## Flight Cancellation

### Purpose

Manage situations where a flight cannot operate and provide passengers with alternative travel options.

### When It Applies

When the airline officially cancels a flight.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, airport staff, and customer support services.

### Required Actions

1. Announce the flight cancellation.
2. Update information displays and reservation systems.
3. Direct passengers to the customer service desk.
4. Offer alternative flights as close as possible to the original departure time.
5. Rebook the ticket if the passenger agrees.
6. If no suitable alternative is available or the passenger declines, process a ticket refund.
7. If the cancellation is the airline's responsibility, provide compensation or vouchers where applicable.
8. Provide meals and hotel accommodation when required.

### Exceptions

Cancellations caused by circumstances beyond the airline's control (such as government flight restrictions) may not qualify for compensation.

### Special Cases

In the event of mass flight cancellations (for example, due to severe weather or strikes), processing may be significantly delayed.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once all passengers have been rebooked or refunded.

## Overbooking

### Purpose

Manage situations where there are more confirmed passengers than available seats on the aircraft.

### When It Applies

When the number of checked-in passengers exceeds the aircraft's seating capacity.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, check-in staff, and overbooking management personnel.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that the flight is oversold.
2. Announce the situation.
3. Request volunteers to give up their seats in exchange for compensation (refund, voucher, meals, hotel accommodation).
4. If there are not enough volunteers, select passengers for involuntary denied boarding (typically the last to check in and those without checked baggage).
5. Arrange alternative flights for affected passengers.
6. Provide compensation in accordance with airline policy and applicable regulations.
7. Document the incident.

### Exceptions

Passengers with certain statuses (such as elite frequent flyer members) may receive priority protection from involuntary denied boarding.

### Special Cases

If a removed passenger has already checked baggage, the baggage must also be removed and returned.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once all confirmed passengers who are not denied boarding have boarded.

## Aircraft Change

### Purpose

Manage changes to the aircraft assigned to a flight.

### When It Applies

When a different aircraft is assigned due to technical issues, scheduling changes, or other operational reasons.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, dispatch services, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Announce the aircraft change.
2. Update information displays.
3. Verify that the replacement aircraft can accommodate all passengers.
4. If the replacement aircraft has fewer seats, overbooking procedures may be required.
5. Check whether seat assignments have changed due to a different cabin layout.
6. Inform passengers of any changes.
7. Assign passengers to appropriate seats on the new aircraft.

### Exceptions

Replacing the aircraft with a larger one typically does not create operational issues.

### Special Cases

If the replacement aircraft cannot accommodate all passengers, overbooking procedures begin.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once all passengers have successfully boarded the replacement aircraft.

## Crew Unavailability

### Purpose

Handle situations where the assigned flight crew is unavailable to operate the flight.

### When It Applies

When pilots or cabin crew cannot report for duty due to reasons such as delays on previous flights, illness, or late arrival.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline, crew scheduling services, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm that the assigned crew is unavailable.
2. Begin searching for a reserve crew.
3. Inform passengers about the delay.
4. If a reserve crew cannot be found within a reasonable time, announce a delay or cancel the flight.
5. Provide passengers with information and support.
6. Handle any resulting rebookings or refunds.

### Exceptions

There are no exceptions. A flight cannot operate without a qualified crew.

### Special Cases

The flight may be delayed for several hours while waiting for a replacement crew from another city.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once a qualified crew is available and the flight departs or is canceled.

## Mass Flight Delays

### Purpose

Manage operations and passenger services during widespread flight disruptions.

### When It Applies

During large-scale operational disruptions (severe weather, airport technical failures, strikes, emergencies).

### Who Is Responsible

Airport management, airlines, and dispatch services.

### Required Actions

1. Assess the scale of the disruption.
2. Activate the mass disruption response plan.
3. Prioritize critical flights.
4. Allocate available resources (staff, waiting areas, food services).
5. Inform passengers through all available communication channels.
6. Organize customer support at service counters.
7. Coordinate actions among airlines.
8. Ensure passengers' basic needs are met (water, food, and information) in waiting areas.

### Exceptions

Standard procedures may be adjusted due to limited available resources.

### Special Cases

Temporary terminal closures or flight diversions may be required.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once normal operations have resumed and all passengers have been assisted.

## Severe Weather

### Purpose

Ensure safety and coordinate operations during adverse weather conditions.

### When It Applies

During fog, snowfall, strong winds, thunderstorms, icing, or any other weather conditions affecting flight safety.

### Who Is Responsible

Airlines, air traffic control, meteorological services, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Obtain weather forecasts and current weather information.
2. Assess whether takeoff and landing operations are safe.
3. Delay flights if necessary.
4. Inform passengers about delays or cancellations.
5. Ensure safety on the apron and runways.
6. Prepare the airport for weather conditions (snow removal, runway treatment).
7. Follow instructions from air traffic control and the airline.

### Exceptions

Weather is considered force majeure and often relieves the airline of liability.

### Special Cases

The airport may need to close temporarily.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once weather conditions improve and normal operations resume.

## Technical Malfunction

### Purpose

Handle situations where an aircraft has a technical issue and cannot operate the scheduled flight.

### When It Applies

When a technical problem is identified before departure or during pre-flight preparations.

### Who Is Responsible

The airline's maintenance department, the flight crew, dispatch services, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Confirm the existence of the technical issue.
2. Assess its severity and whether it can be resolved within a reasonable timeframe.
3. Announce a delay or cancellation.
4. Begin repairs or arrange a replacement aircraft.
5. Inform passengers.
6. Provide passenger support (meals and information).
7. Arrange rebooking or refunds if necessary.

### Exceptions

Some technical issues can be resolved quickly without affecting the schedule.

### Special Cases

If the problem is discovered after boarding, the flight may still be canceled before departure.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the issue has been resolved and the flight departs, or the flight has been canceled.

## Airport Closure

### Purpose

Ensure safety and coordinate actions during a complete airport shutdown.

### When It Applies

### Exceptions

### Special Cases

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the threat has been eliminated and airport operations have resumed.

# 10. Emergency Situations

## Fire

### Purpose

Ensure the safety of people and property while minimizing damage in the event of a fire at the airport.

### When It Applies

When fire, smoke, or a fire alarm is detected.

### Who Is Responsible

Airport fire service, airport personnel, security services, dispatch services, and police.

### Required Actions

1. Upon detecting fire or smoke, immediately activate the fire alarm and notify the airport fire service.
2. Report the location and nature of the incident to the dispatch center.
3. Begin evacuating people from the affected area.
4. Ensure fire crews have access to the incident location.
5. Suspend operations on runways, taxiways, and in the terminal if the fire is in a critical area.
6. Do not use elevators.
7. Follow instructions from airport staff and emergency responders.
8. Provide first aid to injured individuals.
9. Document the incident and begin an investigation after the fire has been extinguished.

### Exceptions

There are no exceptions. Every fire alarm activation is treated as a genuine emergency.

### Special Cases

A fire may occur on an aircraft, on the apron, in the terminal, in a warehouse, or within airport systems.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed after the fire has been fully extinguished, evacuation has been completed, and normal operations have been restored.

## Evacuation

### Purpose

Safely and quickly move all people out of a potentially hazardous area.

### When It Applies

In the event of a bomb threat, fire, chemical leak, terrorist threat, terminal depressurization, or other emergencies.

### Who Is Responsible

Security services, police, fire department, airport staff, and airlines.

### Required Actions

1. Make the decision to evacuate.
2. Activate the emergency notification system.
3. Begin evacuation through the nearest safe exits.
4. Direct people and maintain order.
5. Give evacuation priority to PRM passengers and children.
6. Do not use elevators.
7. Assemble people at the designated assembly point.
8. Conduct a headcount and verify that no one remains inside.
9. Maintain communication with evacuees by providing information and assistance.

### Exceptions

In the event of a limited threat, only part of the facility may be evacuated.

### Special Cases

The evacuation may involve only one terminal, the apron, or the entire airport.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed after the affected area has been inspected and cleared for re-entry, or once the threat has ended.

## Medical Assistance

### Purpose

Provide qualified medical assistance to a passenger or employee in the event of illness or injury.

### When It Applies

When a person experiences illness, injury, childbirth, a heart attack, a stroke, or another medical emergency.

### Who Is Responsible

Airport medical personnel, cabin crew, medically qualified volunteer passengers, and emergency medical services.

### Required Actions

1. Immediately request medical assistance when needed.
2. Ensure responders have access to the patient.
3. Provide first aid if properly trained.
4. Keep the surrounding area calm.

### Special Cases

Medical emergencies on board may require coordination with the flight crew and a possible emergency landing.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed after the necessary medical care has been provided and the patient has been transferred to a hospital or their condition has stabilized.

## Passenger Death

Handle the situation respectfully while ensuring compliance with legal and health regulations.

### When It Applies

When a passenger is declared deceased at the airport or on board an aircraft.

### Who Is Responsible

Medical personnel, police, the airline, and airport staff.

### Required Actions

1. Immediately request medical personnel to officially confirm the death.
2. Notify the police.
3. Secure the incident area.
4. Notify the deceased person's relatives or companions, if applicable.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed after the body and personal belongings have been transferred to the appropriate authorities and the investigation has concluded.

## Bomb Threat

### Purpose

Ensure the safety of people and infrastructure in the event of a bomb threat or the discovery of a suspicious object.

### When It Applies

When a threat is received (phone call, letter, message) or a suspicious object is discovered.

### Who Is Responsible

Security services, police, bomb disposal units, airport staff, and dispatch services.

### Required Actions

1. Record all available information about the threat (time, communication channel, content).

3. Immediately notify airport security.
4. Secure the affected area.

All bomb threats are treated as potentially genuine.

### Special Cases

The threat may target a specific flight, terminal, or the entire airport.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once specialists have assessed the threat and declared the area safe or neutralized the threat.

## Suspicious Object

### Purpose

Ensure safety by isolating and assessing a potentially dangerous object.

### When It Applies

When an unidentified object is found in a public area.

### Who Is Responsible

Any employee who discovers the object, security services, police, and bomb disposal units.

### Required Actions

1. Do not touch the object.
2. Report the discovery to airport security.
3. Secure the area surrounding the object.
4. Prevent unauthorized persons from approaching.
5. Wait for specialists to arrive.

### Special Cases

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once specialists have assessed the object and declared it safe or neutralized it.

The threat may prove to be false, but the response must always be appropriate to the potential risk.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the threat has been eliminated or determined to be unfounded, and normal operations have resumed.

The procedure is completed after the cause has been eliminated and the terminal has been restored to a safe condition.

## Chemical Hazard

Ensure safety in the event of exposure to or the threat of hazardous chemical substances.

### When It Applies

When chemicals leak, hazardous substances are detected, or chemical hazard sensors are activated.

### Who Is Responsible

Security services, hazardous materials response teams, fire department, police, and airport staff.

Dispatch services, technical services, and airport staff.

6. Document the incident.
7. Restore primary communication systems.

### Exceptions

Some operations may be suspended until communications are restored.

### Special Cases

Communication failures may be partial (affecting only certain departments) or complete.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed after all communication systems have been restored and normal communications have resumed.

Ensure safety and maintain minimum airport operations during a power outage.

### When It Applies

When the primary electrical power supply fails.

### Who Is Responsible

Airport technical services, dispatch services, airport staff, and external utility providers.

1. Detect the power outage.
2. Activate backup power sources (diesel generators, UPS systems).
3. Supply power to critical systems (lighting, dispatch centers, security systems, screening checkpoints).
4. Inform passengers and staff about the outage.
5. Suspend non-essential operations.
6. Notify the external utility provider.
7. Monitor the status of backup power systems.
8. Prepare for possible evacuation if backup power is exhausted.
9. Document the incident.

### Exceptions

Some equipment may not function without the primary power supply.

### Special Cases

The outage may be short-term or prolonged, localized or airport-wide.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the primary power supply has been restored and backup systems have been deactivated.

# 12. Working with Passengers

## Conflict Situations

### Purpose

Effectively de-escalate and resolve conflicts with passengers while maintaining safety and professionalism.

### When It Applies

When a passenger expresses dissatisfaction, displays aggressive behavior, refuses to comply with instructions, or behaves disruptively.

### Who Is Responsible

Employees interacting with passengers, security personnel, and the airline.

### Required Actions

1. Remain calm and maintain a professional tone.
2. Listen actively without interrupting the passenger.
3. Show empathy for the passenger's concern: *"I understand that this can be frustrating."*
4. Acknowledge the passenger's feelings.
5. Attempt to resolve the issue or refer the passenger to someone who can assist.
6. If the passenger becomes aggressive, do not respond with aggression.
7. If there is a threat to safety, immediately contact security or the police.
8. Document the incident.

### Exceptions

If the passenger makes physical threats or poses an immediate safety risk, immediately contact law enforcement or security services.

### Special Cases

Conflicts may result from flight delays, lost baggage, operational errors, or the passenger's personal circumstances.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the conflict has been resolved or the passenger has been removed from the service area.

## Complaints

### Purpose

Professionally receive and process passenger complaints to improve service quality and customer satisfaction.

### When It Applies

When a passenger expresses dissatisfaction with service, delays, lost baggage, staff behavior, or similar issues.

### Who Is Responsible

The employee receiving the complaint and customer support services.

### Required Actions

1. Listen carefully without interrupting.
2. Express empathy: *"I'm sorry you experienced these inconveniences."*
3. Confirm your understanding of the issue.
4. If possible, resolve the issue immediately.
5. If the issue cannot be resolved on the spot, register the complaint (PIR, electronic reporting system).
6. Inform the passenger of the next steps and expected resolution timeframe.
7. Record the complaint details for analysis.
8. Follow up to ensure promised actions are completed.

### Exceptions

Complaints containing threats or defamatory statements should be referred to the security service.

### Special Cases

A passenger may insist on an immediate resolution or compensation.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the complaint has been registered or satisfactorily resolved.

## Aggressive Behavior

### Purpose

Ensure the safety of staff and other passengers when dealing with aggressive behavior.

### When It Applies

When a passenger makes threats, behaves abusively, commits physical violence, or demonstrates an intention to cause harm.

### Who Is Responsible

Employees, witnesses, security personnel, and the police.

### Required Actions

1. Do not respond with aggression.
2. Attempt to calm the passenger by speaking calmly.
3. If de-escalation is unsuccessful, move to a safe distance.
4. Immediately contact security or the police.
5. Ensure the safety of other passengers and staff.
6. Document the incident (video, witness statements, incident report).
7. Notify management.

### Exceptions

If there is an immediate threat to safety, use self-defense measures only within legally permitted limits.

### Special Cases

Aggressive behavior may result from alcohol, drugs, mental health conditions, or stress.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the aggressor has been detained or removed and the situation has been stabilized.

## Intoxicated Passengers

### Purpose

Ensure safety and comfort when interacting with passengers under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

### When It Applies

When a passenger is clearly under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

### Who Is Responsible

Employees, the airline, security personnel, and medical staff.

### Required Actions

1. Assess the level of intoxication (slurred speech, impaired walking, aggressive behavior).
2. Determine whether the passenger poses a safety risk.
3. If there is no immediate threat, politely but firmly explain that intoxication is unacceptable.
4. If the passenger poses a threat or refuses to comply, deny boarding or remove the passenger from the flight.
5. Request medical assistance if necessary.
6. In the event of aggression, contact security or the police.
7. Document the incident.

### Exceptions

The airline has the right to refuse transportation to an intoxicated passenger.

### Special Cases

A passenger may attempt to conceal or deny intoxication.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the passenger has been removed from the secure area or has boarded in accordance with operational decisions.

## Language Barrier

### Purpose

Ensure effective communication with passengers who speak other languages.

### When It Applies

When communicating with passengers who do not speak the primary service language.

### Who Is Responsible

All employees interacting with passengers.

### Required Actions

1. Try communicating using simple phrases and gestures.
2. Use online translation tools if available.
3. Seek assistance from a colleague who speaks the required language.
4. Use visual aids (signs, information displays).
5. Use multilingual materials (cards, brochures).
6. Ensure access to information in commonly used languages.

### Exceptions

It is not possible to provide translation services for every language.

### Special Cases

The passenger may be deaf or non-verbal, requiring alternative methods of communication.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once information has been successfully communicated.

## Stressful Situations

### Purpose

Support passengers experiencing stress caused by delays, lost baggage, family circumstances, or similar situations.

### When It Applies

When a passenger shows signs of stress, anxiety, or emotional distress.

### Who Is Responsible

All employees interacting with passengers.

### Required Actions

1. Acknowledge that the passenger is under stress.
2. Speak in a calm and gentle manner.
3. Show empathy and understanding.
4. Offer practical assistance (information, water, a place to sit).
5. Help resolve the issue causing the stress.
6. Do not rush the passenger; allow time to process the information.

### Special Cases

Stress may escalate into panic or aggressive behavior.

### Procedure Completion

The procedure is completed once the passenger's emotional state has stabilized.

# 13. Out of Scope

## What the Bot Can Answer

The AI assistant can answer questions related to:
* Passenger service standards.
* Check-in, boarding, and security screening procedures.
* Baggage handling procedures.
* Aviation security requirements.
* Passport and customs control procedures.
* Procedures for assisting special categories of passengers.
* Response to irregular and emergency situations.
* Coordination between airport services.
* Passenger interaction (conflicts, complaints, stress management).
* Internal airport regulations and policies described in its knowledge base.

## What the Bot Cannot Answer

The AI assistant does **NOT** answer questions related to:
* Financial information (salaries, budgets, accounts).
* Personal passenger information (names, addresses, travel history of specific individuals).
* Confidential incident investigation reports.
* Employment contracts and hiring conditions.
* Internal correspondence or unofficial opinions.
* Legal advice or interpretation of laws.
* Technical equipment documentation (unless included in operational procedures).
* Information about suppliers and commercial contracts.
* Personal data of employees.
* Questions unrelated to airport operations (e.g., "How are you?" or "Tell me a joke.").

## How to Politely Decline

If a question falls outside the bot's area of knowledge, it should:

1. Politely explain that it cannot provide information on the requested topic.
2. Briefly explain the reason for the refusal (e.g., *"This is outside my area of knowledge."* or *"I do not have access to financial information."*).
3. Whenever possible, suggest an alternative source of information (e.g., *"Please contact the Human Resources department for salary information."*).
4. Remain helpful by offering assistance with another question within its area of expertise.

## How to Refer Users to the Appropriate Department

When the bot cannot answer a question, it may direct the passenger or employee to the appropriate department:

* *"For information regarding [topic], please contact the [department name, e.g., 'Customer Support', 'Human Resources', or 'Security Service']."*
* *"This question concerns [subject area] and is best addressed by the [department]."*
* *"You can obtain detailed information from the [department] by phone at [number] or by email at [address]."*

## When to Recommend Speaking with a Staff Member

The bot should recommend contacting an airport employee if:

* The issue requires in-person assistance (e.g., PRM assistance or physical support).
* The situation is unique and is not covered in the knowledge base.
* The situation requires subjective judgment or an exception to standard procedures.
* The passenger or employee specifically requests to speak with a human.
* The issue involves a complaint that should be officially registered.
* Information must be verified, especially in a stressful or emergency situation.

│   └── No

    ├── Yes -> Call security/police. -> End interaction. -> Log the incident.
    └── No -> Proceed with the standard complaint handling procedure.

    ├── Yes -> Process as lost property (lost and found office).

## 16.7. Response to a Bomb Threat

A bomb threat has been received (call, letter, message, witness report).
├── Was the threat received verbally/in writing/online?
│   ├── Yes -> Record the time, channel, and content (if possible).
│   └── No -> Suspicious activity/device has been detected.
├── Notify security services and police immediately.
├── Assess the severity of the threat (detail, source credibility).
├── Activate the appropriate response level (threat response plan).
├── If necessary -> Cordon off the suspicious area.
├── If necessary -> Initiate evacuation.
├── If necessary -> Call the bomb squad.
├── Coordinate actions with external services.
├── Inform passengers and staff (within reasonable limits).
└── Document all actions.

## 16.9. Passenger Under the Influence of Alcohol or Drugs

## 16.10. Fire Discovery

The fire alarm has been triggered or fire/smoke has been detected.

├── Follow instructions from staff and rescue personnel.
├── Provide first aid to the injured.
└── Log the incident and begin an investigation after the fire is extinguished.

# 17. Practical Scenarios

## Scenario 1: Flight Delay Due to Technical Malfunction

**Context:** Flight SU1234 from Moscow to Paris is delayed by 4 hours due to a hydraulic system malfunction.

**Actions:**
1.  The airline dispatcher informs airport staff and updates the display boards.
2.  An airline employee makes an announcement: "Dear passengers of flight SU1234 to Paris, the flight is delayed for an indefinite period due to a technical malfunction. We apologize for the inconvenience. Please monitor the display boards and our counters for updates."
3.  Passengers are notified that beverages and snacks will be provided.
4.  When the delay is confirmed to exceed 5 hours, the airline begins issuing hotel vouchers for those interested.
5.  After the malfunction is resolved, the flight departs with a delay, and passengers board the aircraft.

## Scenario 2: Passenger with Reduced Mobility (PRM)

**Context:** An elderly woman using a wheelchair arrives at the airport for an international flight.

**Actions:**
1.  The "WCHC" (wheelchair to cabin chair) requirement was specified during booking.
2.  An airport employee meets the passenger at the entrance and provides an airport wheelchair.
3.  The passenger is escorted to the check-in counter, where their luggage is accepted and the boarding pass is handed to them.
4.  During screening, the escort assists the passenger through the metal detector and provides information about the passenger to the screening officer.
5.  At the boarding gate, jetbridge/bus staff meet the passenger and help them reach the aircraft.
6.  On board, a flight attendant assists with seating and confirms whether additional help is needed.
7.  Upon arrival at the destination, an escort meets the passenger and assists with deplaning and passing through passport control.

## Scenario 3: Suspicious Bag Found in the Waiting Area

**Context:** Airport security is notified about a forgotten bag in a public area.

**Actions:**
1.  An employee spots the bag, with no one nearby.
2.  The employee does not touch the bag, steps back, and reports the finding via radio to the security duty desk.
3.  Security arrives at the scene, cordons off the area, and keeps people away.
4.  The bomb squad is called.
5.  Passengers and staff nearby are evacuated from the danger zone.
6.  The bomb squad inspects the bag and determines it contains personal belongings of an owner who walked away.
7.  The bag is handed over to the lost and found office. The cordon is lifted.

## Scenario 4: Aggressive Passenger Behavior During a Flight Delay

**Context:** A male passenger, late for an important business meeting, starts yelling at an airline employee because of the flight delay.

**Actions:**
1.  The employee remains calm and does not respond with aggression.
2.  "I understand that this delay is causing you inconvenience, and I am sorry."
3.  The passenger continues to shout and make threats.
4.  The employee politely but firmly says: "Please lower your voice. If you do not calm down, I will have to call security."
5.  The passenger does not respond.
6.  The employee calls security.
7.  Security arrives, intervenes, and removes the passenger from the service area.
8.  The incident is logged in the system.

## Scenario 5: Lost Baggage

**Context:** A passenger arrives from a vacation and does not find their suitcase on the baggage claim carousel.

**Actions:**
1.  The passenger approaches the "Lost Baggage" counter.
2.  The employee asks for the baggage tag number and the passenger's details.
3.  The passenger provides the tag and passport.
4.  The employee enters the data into the WorldTracer system.
5.  A Property Irregularity Report (PIR) is created.
6.  The employee informs the passenger that the baggage is being traced and that they will be notified once it is found.
7.  The passenger is given contact information for tracking purposes.
8.  Two days later, the baggage is found at another airport and delivered to the passenger.

## Scenario 6: In-Flight Medical Assistance

**Context:** During the flight, a female passenger feels unwell, with a high fever and dizziness.

**Actions:**
1.  The passenger presses the call button.
2.  A flight attendant brings the first aid kit, provides initial assistance (measures blood pressure, temperature).
3.  Asks the passenger if they have any medication, flight experience, or chronic conditions.
4.  The flight attendant makes a PA announcement: "Is there a doctor or medical professional on board?"
5.  A volunteer passenger who is a doctor is found.
6.  The doctor examines the patient and confirms the need for medical attention.
7.  The flight attendant informs the captain.
8.  The captain decides on a diversion to the nearest suitable airport.
9.  At the airport, a medical team meets the aircraft, and the patient is hospitalized.

## Scenario 7: Bomb Threat (False Alarm)

**Context:** The airport receives an anonymous call threatening a bomb in Terminal B.

**Actions:**
1.  The call is recorded and forwarded to the security service.
2.  The bomb threat response plan is activated.
3.  The suspicious area (Terminal B) is cordoned off.
4.  Partial evacuation of Terminal B begins.
5.  The bomb squad and police are called.
6.  A thorough search of the building is conducted.
7.  The threat is determined to be a false alarm after verification.
8.  The cordon is lifted, and passengers are allowed back into the terminal.
9.  An investigation into the incident is initiated.

## Scenario 8: Unaccompanied Minor (UMNR)

**Context:** An 8-year-old boy is flying to another city to visit his grandmother.

**Actions:**
1.  The parents arrange the UMNR service with the airline at the time of booking.
2.  On the day of departure, the parents accompany the boy to the check-in counter.
3.  The airline employee confirms the booking, checks documents (birth certificate), and the UMNR form.
4.  The employee escorts the boy to the boarding gate and hands him over to the flight attendant along with the escort form.
5.  The flight attendant seats the boy, supervises him, and assists with meals and entertainment.
6.  Upon arrival, the flight attendant hands the boy over to the authorized person (grandmother), verifying the greeter's documents.

## Scenario 9: Border Control – Document Error

**Context:** A passenger arrives from the Schengen area, but their passport has a printing error in the date of birth (one digit is wrong).

**Actions:**
1.  The border guard checks the passport and notices the error.
2.  Verifies the name and photo – they match.
3.  The border guard asks the passenger for documents confirming identity (driver's license, birth certificate).
4.  The passenger provides a birth certificate with the correct date.
5.  The border guard consults with a superior officer.
6.  A decision is made: the passenger is allowed through with a verbal warning and a recommendation to correct the passport.
7.  The incident is logged.

## Scenario 10: Flight Overbooking

**Context:** The flight is fully booked, but more passengers with confirmed tickets arrive than there are seats available in the cabin.

**Actions:**
1.  During boarding, it becomes clear that more passengers are attempting to board than there are seats.
2.  The airline employee announces: "Dear passengers, our flight is overbooked. We are looking for volunteers willing to take the next flight in exchange for compensation (refund, voucher, meals, hotel)."
3.  A sufficient number of volunteers come forward.
4.  The remaining passengers board without issue.
5.  The volunteers receive compensation and are rebooked on the next flight.
6.  If there are not enough volunteers, the airline selects passengers for involuntary denial of boarding (those who checked in last, those without baggage) and applies the same compensation measures.
